Formal Association Mining Metrics

1. Support (P(A ∩ B))
Proportion of movies in the catalog containing both genre A and genre B.

Support(A → B) = Count(A ∪ B) / Total Movies

Indicates the statistical significance and frequency of the co-occurrence.
2. Confidence (P(B | A))
Conditional probability that a movie has genre B given that it is tagged with genre A.

Confidence(A → B) = Support(A ∪ B) / Support(A)

Reflects directional predictive reliability.
3. Lift (Ratio of Dependence)
Measures how much more often A and B occur together than expected if independent.

Lift(A → B) = Confidence(A → B) / Support(B)

Lift > 1: Positive association.
Lift = 1: Independent occurrence.

Complete Mined Association Rules Table

Key Mining Discovery
The strongest mutual association in the entire dataset is between Children's and Animation: An antecedent of Children's yields Animation with a massive Lift of 12.38 and Confidence of 83.2%. Conversely, Animation implies Children's with the exact same Lift of 12.38 and a Confidence of 64.4%. This demonstrates near-complete industrial coupling of animated feature films with family and children's content during this era.

🧬 Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

Unsupervised dimensionality reduction projecting 6,040 user taste vectors across 18 genre dimensions into a 2D preference segment map.

Mathematical Formulation of the User-Genre Preference Matrix
How high-dimensional viewing behaviors were reduced into latent components
1. Feature Matrix Construction: Aggregated 1,000,209 ratings into a 6,040 × 18 matrix where each cell represents user u's average rating for genre g (unrated genres filled with 0).
2. Z-Score Standardization: Standardized using StandardScaler() (zero mean, unit variance) to prevent high-volume genres from artificially dominating covariance.
3. Orthogonal Eigen-Decomposition: Computed top 2 eigenvectors capturing maximum orthogonal variance across the user population.

Formal Project Conclusion

DMT Synthesis Summary
The MovieLens 1M dataset was systematically investigated using Data Mining Techniques. By integrating exploratory profiling, combinatorial genre pairs, Apriori frequent itemset mining, and unsupervised PCA dimensionality reduction:
  • Identified the universal positive rating skew and benchmarked the global average at 3.58.
  • Uncovered the contrast between mass commercial genres (Comedy/Drama) and elite niche acclaim (Film-Noir/Documentary).
  • Established quantitative co-occurrence rules with statistical lift up to 12.38x.
  • Demonstrated that user genre preferences reduce cleanly to 2 principal components distinguishing omnivorous breadth from visceral spectacle.
All analyses and metrics presented are verified against the raw dataset records and reproducible pipelines.

Formal Algorithm & Hyperparameter Specifications

Apriori Association Rule Mining
Frequent itemset discovery in transactional movie genres
  • Domain: Movie catalog multi-genre combinations (3,883 transactions × 18 items).
  • Implementation: mlxtend.frequent_patterns.apriori & association_rules.
  • Minimum Support (s): 0.02 (at least 2.0% of all catalog movies).
  • Evaluation Metric: Confidence (threshold ≥ 0.30).
  • Ranking Criterion: Lift factor (descending).
  • Verification: Highest lift achieved: 12.38 for Children's ↔ Animation.
Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
Unsupervised dimensional decomposition of user preference space
  • Input Matrix (X): 6,040 users × 18 genres rating matrix (unrated = 0).
  • Standardization: StandardScaler() centering mean to 0 and variance to 1 per genre.
  • Decomposition: sklearn.decomposition.PCA(n_components=2) via SVD.
  • Explained Variance: PC1 = 26.8%; PC2 = 11.8% (Cumulative: 38.6%).
  • Semantic Axes: PC1 captures general engagement breadth; PC2 captures visceral spectacle vs character drama.

Dataset Limitations & Academic Citation

Dataset Limitations
Important contextual factors for interpreting findings
  • Historical Snapshot: Data collected in 2000 reflecting late-1990s viewing habits and internet access patterns.
  • Self-Selection Bias: Users voluntarily choose which movies to rate, creating positive skewness (mean 3.58).
  • Self-Reported Demographics: Age, Gender, Occupation provided voluntarily without verification.
  • Discrete Scale: Ratings are whole stars from 1 to 5, without half-star increments or textual reviews.
